\section{Derivatives}
The derivative of a function $f$ at $x_0$ is defined as the limit
\[ f'(x_0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x_0+h) - f(x_0)}{h}. \]
It measures the instantaneous rate of change. Differentiability implies
continuity, but not the converse.

\subsection{Chain rule}
For composite functions, $(f \circ g)'(x) = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x)$.

\section{Integration}
The fundamental theorem of calculus links differentiation and integration:
\[ \int_a^b f'(x)\,dx = f(b) - f(a). \]
